# Academic Writing

## Structure

- **Abstract** — 150–250 words: problem, method, key result, implication. Written last.
- **Introduction** — context → gap → thesis → roadmap. State your claim precisely in one sentence.
- **Body** — one argument per section; every paragraph makes a claim, supports it with evidence, and links back to the thesis.
- **Conclusion** — synthesize findings; state limitations and future work. Do not introduce new claims.

## Style Rules

1. **Precision over flourish.** Use exact terms; define specialist vocabulary on first use.
2. **Active voice where honest.** "We analyzed the data" unless the field convention demands passive.
3. **Hedge appropriately.** "The results suggest" not "This proves" — match claim strength to evidence.
4. **One idea per paragraph**, with a clear topic sentence.
5. **Cite every factual claim** that is not your own reasoning or common knowledge.
6. Avoid contractions, rhetorical questions, and emotive language.

## Citation Integrity

- Paraphrase substantively; quote verbatim only when wording matters.
- Every in-text citation must appear in the reference list, and vice versa.
- Never cite a source you have not actually read — cite the original, not "as cited in."

## Revision Passes

1. Argument pass — does every section serve the thesis?
2. Paragraph pass — topic sentence first, logical flow between paragraphs?
3. Sentence pass — cut filler ("very", "in order to", "it should be noted that")?
4. Citation pass — check all claims and references match?

## Self-Check Before Submitting

- Can a reader state your thesis in one sentence after reading the introduction?
- Is every claim either argued or cited?
- Does the abstract accurately reflect the actual content?
