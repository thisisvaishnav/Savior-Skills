# Source Evaluation Checklist

Rate every source before using it in research output.

## Credibility
- [ ] Who is the author? Do they have relevant credentials or affiliations?
- [ ] Is the publisher reputable (university, government, established outlet)?
- [ ] Is this a primary source (original data/statement) or secondary (someone else's summary)?

## Currency
- [ ] When was it published or last updated?
- [ ] Is the information still accurate, or superseded by newer findings?

## Accuracy & Bias
- [ ] Are claims supported by evidence, data, or citations within the source?
- [ ] Does the source have an obvious agenda, sponsorship, or conflict of interest?
- [ ] Do at least two independent sources agree on the key claim?

## Verdict
- **Use directly** — primary, credible, current, corroborated.
- **Use with caveat** — credible but secondary or slightly dated.
- **Discard** — anonymous, promotional, unverifiable, or contradicted elsewhere.

# Citation Format

Use this format in research summaries:

```
- [Claim or finding] — [Source Title], [Author/Org] ([Year]). URL
```

Example:

> Global EV sales grew 35% year-over-year in 2025 — IEA Global EV Outlook (2026). https://www.iea.org/reports/global-ev-outlook-2026
